Three reasons to hold
  1. Material accuracy risk. Outdated Enterprise pricing ($49 instead of $59) can mislead decisions during a rollout to the top 50 enterprise accounts. The case frames $50K+ annual subscriptions as at risk; it does not quantify realized losses.
  2. Limited validation. Rule check, safety rule, and judge each caught 1/1 P0 cases. Three checks on one case are not three independent pricing examples.
  3. Readiness remains unproven. The listed evaluation costs total $192,000. Implementation, expanded test coverage, and monitoring evidence are still needed.
Proposed next step

Request a temporary release hold. Engineering and AI Quality would expand pricing validation by November 27, 2026, followed by a ship-or-hold review on November 30, 2026. These are proposed dates, subject to owner agreement.

Evidence required to reconsider

Run known outdated pricing cases and verified-correct controls; check source freshness, unsupported formats, and unavailable references. Require 100% detection of known outdated prices, zero false alarms on verified-correct controls, and no missed P0 pricing hallucinations. Record case counts, misses, false alarms, source versions, and owner sign-off.

Module 1
Evaluation strategy
User promise

Give enterprise strategists and product leaders fast, accurate, verifiable market intelligence backed by trusted sources.

Three trust metrics
  • Hallucination rate: materially incorrect or unsupported answers as a share of evaluated answers.
  • Latency: response time in seconds; the course example uses a 2.0-second target.
  • Robustness: ability to handle ambiguous or complex questions and missing evidence appropriately.
Trade-offs

Prioritize factual accuracy and safe handling of uncertainty when they require more time. Track the latency cost of validation so the trade-off stays visible. A refusal that follows policy should not count as a robustness failure.

Measurable release criteria
  • Hard: 100% detection of known outdated pricing cases and zero false alarms on verified-correct controls. Verify reference freshness. Any missed P0 pricing hallucination blocks release.
  • Soft: 0% unreviewed material inaccuracies in competitive comparisons; 100% corrected or covered by a documented Product-approved override.
  • Advisory: response time ≤ 2.0 seconds per evaluated response. A miss triggers investigation and prioritization.
If a soft gate fails

Correct the issue or obtain a documented Product-approved exception. A proposed rollout to 5% of users may proceed only after the hard gate passes and monitoring, rollback criteria, and ownership are defined. Limited exposure does not override the P0 pricing gate.

Insights

What I learned

Where the course was hardest, what you’ll keep doing, and the moment your thinking changed.

Friction

The hardest part was turning limited eval results into a defensible release decision: separating frequency from severity, choosing measurable thresholds, and assigning ownership.

Key learnings

Define failure modes early, connect metrics to business impact, and set release gates before testing. Reassess coverage as the product evolves and distinguish observed results from proposed controls.

Aha moment

Catching 1 out of 1 critical failures is a useful first signal. It does not establish real-world reliability; sample size and verified-correct controls matter.
